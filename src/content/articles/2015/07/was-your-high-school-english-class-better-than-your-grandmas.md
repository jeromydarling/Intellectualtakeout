My grandmother passed away a few months ago. While looking through her belongings, I discovered several scrapbooks from her school days in the 1930s and 40s.

One page in the scrapbook focuses on her 9th-grade English course in a small, rural high school in Minnesota. Pasted to the page is a workbook of sample questions which she apparently used to prepare for the state’s year-end exam.

![](https://chroniclesmagazine-legacy.ae-admin.com/assets/3/28/1942english1.jpg)

The little booklet is filled with questions on everything from spelling to grammar to literature.

While pondering the titles regularly referred to in the literature section, it suddenly dawned on me where my grandmother got the sophisticated and descriptive writing style that permeates her scrapbooks. In all likelihood, it was fostered by the high-end literature she was expected to study even in her small, country school. Simply reading the first paragraphs of each of these titles – and running them through a [text analyzer](https://www.renaissance.com/products/accelerated-reader/atos-analyzer) – reveal books with complex vocabulary and syntax, and reading levels generally several grades beyond what we might expect from today’s 9th-grade English students:

[**_The Lady of the Lake_**](http://www.gutenberg.org/files/3011/3011-h/3011-h.htm) **– Sir Walter Scott** (Reading Level\* – 13.0)  
[**_The Vision of Sir Launfal_**](http://www.gutenberg.org/files/17119/17119-h/17119-h.htm) **– James Russell Lowell** (Reading Level – 13.0)  
[**_Lincoln, a Man of the People_**](http://www.poetryfoundation.org/poem/182326) **– Edwin Markham** (Reading Level – 7.6)  
[**_My Native Land_**](http://www.poemhunter.com/poem/my-native-land/) **– Sir Walter Scott** (Reading Level – 14.0)  
[**_The Gold Bug_**](http://xroads.virginia.edu/~hyper/POE/gold_bug.html) **– Edgar Allan Poe** (Reading Level – 12.0)  
[**_To a Waterfowl_**](http://www.poets.org/poetsorg/poem/waterfowl) **– William Cullen Bryant** (Reading Level – 13.0)  
[**_The Ballad of East and West_**](http://www.bartleby.com/246/1129.html) **– Rudyard Kipling** (Reading Level – 11.0)  
[**_The Chambered Nautilus_**](http://www.poetryfoundation.org/poem/173644) **– Oliver Wendell Holmes. Sr.** (Reading Level – 10.0)  
[**_The Ransom of Red Chief_**](http://fiction.eserver.org/short/ransom_of_red_chief.html) **– O. Henry** (Reading Level – 11.0)  
[**_The Great Stone Face_**](http://www.online-literature.com/hawthorne/139/) **– Nathaniel Hawthorne** (Reading Level – 7.0)  
(\*Reading level numbers correspond with grades: 9.0 = 9th grade, etc.)

Here and there, today’s public schools will offer one or two classic selections like these. But more often than not, students are given titles which are [minimally challenging](http://www.better-ed.org/blog/middle-school-reading-lists-100-years-ago-vs-today).

I wonder… would we see so many millennials struggling to read and write if today’s students were held to the same high standards as my grandmother was?

I sincerely doubt it.
