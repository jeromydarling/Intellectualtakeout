Here is a look at the top compensated college presidents:

![Richest College Presidents](/sites/ito/files/richest_college_presidents.png)

These numbers might be staggering, but they are actually down considerably from recent years.

In 2010, for example, former Governor and U.S. Senator Bob Kerrey took home a whopping $3.05 million as president of [New School](http://www.newschool.edu/) in New York City, Forbes [reported at the time](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tomvanriper/2012/12/10/the-highest-paid-college-presidents/#7364636c13da). Several other presidents earned annual packages in excess of $2 million that year.

The Chronicle’s latest figures breakdown salaries into four categories: total compensation, base pay, bonus pay, and other pay. (Just [hover over the graphic in the article](http://www.chronicle.com/interactives/executive-compensation?cid=db&elqTrackId=bbbeb5de58fa4e8bb542a09def4f5281&elq=2435f72c05734fbc8056e8cb9668ca86&elqaid=14546&elqat=1&elqCampaignId=6134#id=table_public_2016)  to get the summary.)

Eight of the presidents in the top ten had six-figure bonuses. William H. McRaven, president of the University of Texas System, claimed the biggest bonus: $300,000, one-fourth of his base pay.  

According to the Federal Reserve, median income of an American household was $56,516 in 2015.

According to U.S. Census Bureau statistics, Americans with doctorates earn income of roughly $81,400. The average for an advanced degree is $72,824.
