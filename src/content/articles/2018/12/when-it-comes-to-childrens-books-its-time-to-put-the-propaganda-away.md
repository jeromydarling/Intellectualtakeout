While many of America’s parents are worried about how well their children can read and do math, as well how much they know, [the helpful people at Lifehacker](https://www.adl.org/education-and-resources/resources-for-educators-parents-families/childrens-literature?childrens-lit-select-all-3=1&tid%5B171%5D=171&tid%5B172%5D=172&tid%5B173%5D=173&tid%5B174%5D=174&tid%5B175%5D=175&tid%5B217%5D=217&tid%5B218%5D=218&tid%5B219%5D=219&tid%5B220%5D=220&tid%5B221%5D=221&childrens-lit-select-all-8=1) are concerned about what we should to do teach social justice.

They have recommended a list of books to ensure that our children are thinking correctly–not, mind you, that they are thinking well, but only that they are taking the right political and social thoughts.

Think your children should be learning about our nation’s founding and the basis for our republic. NOT. Why do that when your children could be reading _Hillary Rodham Clinton: Some Girls are Born to Lead_. Never seen that one on the great books list? Maybe they needed something to balance the books on Ronald Reagan. Oh, wait, there are no books about Ronald Reagan on the Lifehacker list. In fact, there are none on any conservative figure. Just Hillary. 

And forget about [_Little House on the Prairie_](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0064400409/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0064400409&linkId=eef124e96e9047b6998b8950c721cfc2) or [_Anne of Green Gables_](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B0012R2GXY/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=B0012R2GXY&linkId=667d3aafef6977c12302d66f83fa8820). Laura Ingalls Wilder is racist. Hadn’t you heard? And L. M. Montgomery was clearly a shill for the Patriarchy. Instead, try _Rad Women Worldwide_. This one even has “cut-paper portraits” of radical, revolutionary women. Who needs books that teach you how to think when you can teach kids how to protest and chant slogans?

Don’t bother with Virginia Lee Burton’s [_Mike Mulligan and His Steam Shovel_](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0395259398/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0395259398&linkId=2ea07e86cbce2d9583e79026ef36228d), or Bill Beet’s classic dog story, [_The Wingdingdilly_](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0395313813/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0395313813&linkId=8127ab160dab6d7911fc8d9160ea86ad), or any other book that most boys would actually, you know, _like_. No. How about _Sparkle Boy_? a book about cross dressing for that active boy in your family?

There are other books on the list covering racism, sexism, ableism, LGBTQ issues, and immigration.

But if our cultural commissars had any familiarity with classic children’s and young adult literature, they would know that many of these issues are already addressed in these books, and addressed better than preachy, partisan texts designed to indoctrinate children. You can teach children important social issues and familiarize them with their history and literary heritage at the same time.

Want to innoculate your younger child against racism? Have them read [_Call me Charley_](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B000NPI2EI/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=B000NPI2EI&linkId=1894643b1bbe1662eef9bd0557554eee), by Jesse Jackson. Yes, _that_ Jesse Jackson. Older children can read [_To Kill a Mockingbird_](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0060935464/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0060935464&linkId=02e93cd54a86110dd73bbc89bd070941) and _[Cry, The Beloved Country](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0743262174/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0743262174&linkId=8453704b8891f42972a67864e10836f1)._ They are beautiful stories that address racism from a _human_, not a _political_ viewpoint. 

Want to read about strong women? Young readers can read about Betsy Ray in Maud Hart Lovelace’s endearing [_Betsy-Tacy_](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0062095870/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0062095870&linkId=844ca3db4e7e41c365352f72e547141e) stories or [_Sarah, Plain and Tall_](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0062399527/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0062399527&linkId=b42d67b71abab0dfa56d92efa81c55f5). And older readers should try Beryl Markham’s _West with the Night_, the classic story of the woman who was the first person to fly east to west across the Atlantic.

And for immigration, try [_Mama’s Bank Account_](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0156563770/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0156563770&linkId=e668246b6d20d8aef767f01f9d33ec79) by Kathryn Forbes, the book that was the basis for the classic film, “I Remember Mama.” Or the wonderful [_Papa’s Wife_](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0553243470/ref=as_li_qf_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0553243470&linkId=b0628ded94aea06f6674885bf109d63d), by Thyra Ferre Bjorn.

Put the propaganda aside. There is a right way to familiarize students with important social issues through real literature.
