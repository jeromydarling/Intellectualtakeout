Whenever I hear that [only 12 percent](http://www.nationsreportcard.gov/) of American students are proficient in history, I have to shake my head in amazement. How in the world can so few students be proficient in a subject that’s so fascinating?

Historian [David McCullough](/blog/david-mccullough-whats-wrong-history-education-today) may have an answer to that question. Several years ago he noted that contemporary history textbooks are:

> “\[S\]o badly written. They’re boring! Historians are never required to write for people other than historians. Most of them are doing excellent work. I draw on their excellent work. I admire some of them more than anybody I know. But, by and large, they haven’t learned to write very well.”

Such may have been one of the reasons why my mother hated history as a child in school.

Fortunately, her hatred of history was not passed down to me because she followed a simple piece of advice. Beginning in kindergarten, we read historical fiction books together. The more we read, the more we both grew to love history. By the time I was actually old enough to hit the history textbooks, I was able to happily endure their “boring” nature because my appetite had already been whet.

As was recently noted, [Americans are embracing bad government because they don’t know history](/blog/americans-are-embracing-bad-government-because-they-dont-know-history). If we want our children to avoid this fate, then perhaps we should start following the advice given to my mom years ago. Here are a few authors and titles to set your child on a history-loving path:

**Ingri and Edgar D’Aulaire**  
The D’Aulaire’s colorfully illustrated biographies read like a storybook and introduce children to diverse historical figures such as [Leif Erikson](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0816695458/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0816695458&linkId=0466bac968c507146c077d2d31085451), [Pocahontas](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/1893103285/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=1893103285&linkId=541968ea98b4c870cf449c411aef0237), and [Abraham Lincoln](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/1893103609/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=1893103609&linkId=e3d5b147ce420ea1d02a3671a037e86e).

**Robert Lawson**  
What kid doesn’t enjoy a story told from the perspective of an animal? Robert Lawson’s use of anthropomorphism presents the stories of [Benjamin Franklin](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0812422058/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0812422058&linkId=b5552d723b546c5e619cc8a186c9dbce) and [Paul Revere](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0316517291/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0316517291&linkId=9dfa0659b6679572a6e8fd50dd9cfdbe) with a hilarity captivating to children and adults alike.

Henty’s books appeal to boys and girls alike, and cover almost every major time period in history from [ancient Egypt](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0486423638/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0486423638&linkId=2a47f67187397f8e99d7159e33424efc) to the [Crusades](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/1535384891/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=1535384891&linkId=529b5244cb93ce96eedf28a70af70a0d) and on to the [French Revolution](https://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0486466043/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=intelltakeo0d-20&camp=1789&creative=9325&linkCode=as2&creativeASIN=0486466043&linkId=19aa533bb58479ff28539934f41ba47a).
