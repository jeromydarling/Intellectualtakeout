Conservatives and liberals disagree vehemently because both sides of the political aisle rely on [different standards of morality](/2026/11/liberal-conservative-morality/). Both sides are convinced of their own righteousness and of the depravity of the other; both sides expect the other to conform to the opposite moral paradigm.

The result, as many have observed, is that conservatives and liberals talk past each other rather than to each other. Both parties are unable to fathom how someone could ever vote for the opposite side, concluding that their political opponents are immoral – or worse, amoral.

In reality, most people have a clear sense of right and wrong and make a real effort to behave morally. They simply measure morality by different metrics.

Yet there is a distinct “conservative advantage” when trying to win someone over with a political argument, psychologist Jonathan Haidt says. Haidt does not indicate that the conservative position is morally superior, nor that conservatives can always claim the moral high ground; rather, it means that conservative arguments appeal to more foundations for morality than liberal arguments do.

**What is Moral Foundations Theory?**

Haidt presents his Moral Foundations Theory in his 2012 book “[The Righteous Mind](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B078Z29HSP?lv=shuf&hvlocphy=9019550&hvptwo=&hvnetw=g&mcid=&hvext=373621214456&hvadid=814831265899&language=en_US&hvpone=&hvlocint=&hvcampaign=23942094407&hydadcr=16529_13842034_2502596&hvpos=&hvdev=c&hvdvcmdl=&hvocijid=18227142328232920512--&hvqmt=a&hvexpln=0&tag=smgglusdsadts-20&hvtargid=kwl-2484167636399&adgrpid=203685377651&hvrand=18227142328232920512&channelId=500&ref_=&plpRedirect=mhFallback)_._” All human societies have a code of moral conduct, and while they may differ in practice (some countries view criticism of political authority as reprehensible, while others have moralistic standards of dress or diet) all standards of morality are based on at least one of the following moral foundations: Care, Fairness, Loyalty, Authority, and Sanctity. Societies view actions as right or wrong based on their adherence or opposition to these moral ideals.

In 2009, Haidt, who was socially liberal at the time, sought to understand why people voted for conservatives when their positions, in the eyes of most liberals, were morally inferior at best. Alongside others, Haidt [conducted a survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3116962/#S20) in which thousands indicated their political affiliation, responding to various scenarios with indications of how much Care, Fairness, etc. were relevant to their judgment of an action as moral or immoral.

### **Conservative Arguments Appeal to a Wider Range of Moral Foundations**

The findings were telling. Respondents from across the political spectrum rated Care and Fairness as the most important criteria. However, conservatives appealed to Loyalty, Authority, and Sanctity significantly more than their liberal counterparts, with the very liberal ranking these foundations as the least important and the very conservative as the most important.

When marketing their political beliefs, liberals all but ignore these latter three moral foundations, while conservatives appeal to all five in similar degrees.

### **Care vs. Harm** 

Thanks to our (very American) understanding of personal liberty and independence, we prioritize care for others above all else. If something causes harm to someone, most of us classify it as morally bad.

We see this repeatedly in liberal slogans like “my body, my choice” and “love is love,” with the undercurrent idea being that no one should oppose something that does not directly harm him. Conservatives may reach different conclusions about these positions but still appeal to the Care foundation; for example, a common rebuttal for pro-choice arguments is that abortion is harmful to women. Care is important to both political parties even when they reach different conclusions about what is most kind and most cruel.

### **Fairness vs. Cheating**

Fairness is also obvious to both political parties. Liberals who support socialist-leaning policies or oppose billionaires do so in the name of fairness. After all, if there is enough wealth to go around, it is unfair for it to be concentrated in the hands of the few. The welfare state is another extension of the argument for fairness. Liberals are also more likely to define equality as equality of outcome, because the alternative is unfair.

Conservatives also care deeply about fairness. For them, equality of opportunity is ideal; it provides mobility for all but (in a perfect world) lets those who work hardest reap the benefits of their labor. This is why conservatives are opposed to the welfare state and benefits for illegal immigrants; for them, fairness is focused more on rewarding good behavior.

### **Loyalty vs. Betrayal**

If you see a house that flies an American flag, it’s a safe bet that the owner leans conservative. Conservatives feel attached to a national identity and prize loyalty to their “team.”

Liberals, however, may find patriotism embarrassing or even wrong. A growing number of liberals [refuse to celebrate Independence Day](https://www.the-independent.com/news/world/americas/us-politics/america250-independence-trump-divisions-pennsylvania-b3004933.html) because they do not want to approve the current direction of the United States. Likewise, because many of them do not wish to tout the United States as a superior country (perhaps in the name of equality), they are less likely to approve of foreign policy. As a result, they struggle to connect with voters because Loyalty is not a central foundation to their policy.

### **Authority vs. Subversion**

Favoring a strong government and being more traditionally religious are key components of conservatism, which appeals to the Authority foundation of morality. The Authority foundation views hierarchy as a good because of its role in creating an ordered society. Thus, unjust insubordination is viewed as a threat, while respect for authority (both political and religious) is a virtue.

“\[I\]t is much easier for the political right to build on this foundation than it is for the left, which often defines itself in part by its opposition to hierarchy, inequality, and power,” Haidt writes. The liberal idolization of equality can make the left hostile to authority.

### **Sanctity vs. Degradation**

Sanctity does not refer only to religious ideals, but also to views of sacredness. Many Americans view flags, religious symbols, and principles as sacred. Conservatives, however, tend to use these principles as foundations for morality.

“American conservatives are more likely to talk about ‘the sanctity of life’ or ‘the sanctity of marriage,’” Haidt writes. Protection of life, marriage, and the body as a temple are often key for conservatives when determining morality, while sanctity is often not a consideration for liberals.

These five foundations of morality are universal and appear in some form or another in many societies. Appealing to one or more of these foundations is often innate in a society, but also strategic when trying to win others over. While the “conservative advantage” may hold true for the moment, conservatives must continue to prize these five foundations if they want to maintain influence over ideas and ideals.
