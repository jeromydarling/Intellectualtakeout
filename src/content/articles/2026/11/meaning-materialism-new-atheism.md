It should surprise no one that the [New Atheism movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Atheism) is in sharp decline. Along with the fact that it was driven more by rhetoric than by logic, it also left in its wake an acute emptiness even amongst its devotees.

This is no accident; the snide, skeptical, and sarcastic undermining of the spiritual inevitably and unsurprisingly leaves little substance after all things pertaining to the soul are stripped away.

My criticism of New Atheism in general is concerned less with its tone and more with the philosophical materialism that it assumes, however. Materialism, or the belief that only material realities exist, eliminates all that allows for meaning in existence. This is not just my emotions talking. Meaning, by its nature, is an immaterial substance that relies upon immaterial realities to exist.

This is true even under the assumption that meaning is purely arbitrary. Consider the human person: All would agree that in order to attribute meaning to something, that person has to have a sense of self. This self relies upon memory of one’s past experiences. Memory, however, is not made of stuff that can be observed or measured. It is immaterial.

Yes, we can observe brain cells and synapses, and we can measure brain waves and responses to stimuli, but not even the most fervent materialist would equate these with memory or self. They only provide indication of a memory (and by extension, self) that is already there.

This is because the sense of self, and the memory upon which it is based, are extensions of the rational soul. While it is technically possible for a materialist to accept the existence of a soul, as the word “soul” refers to the principle of life present in every living thing, that same materialist would reject the persistence of the soul after the death of the body.

However, the same faculties that make memories and sense of self real and persistent are only present in a soul that holds onto immaterial realities and persists independent of a physical body.

In fact, the faculties that recognize and determine meaning, the intellect and the will, are classically known as the two powers of the rational soul.

The intellect’s [ability to recognize meaning](/2025/12/what-makes-us-happy/) comes from its capacity to understand abstract concepts. If one assumes the premise that meaning exists independently and must be sought and found by each person, then meaning itself and our ability to find it are both immaterial realities.

Maybe one assumes a different premise about meaning, that each person arbitrarily attributes meaning to something. This too relies on the other power of the rational soul that proceeds from the intellect: the will. If one believes that meaning only comes from the person who gives meaning, then meaning still requires an immaterial, rational soul to decide to give that meaning.

If there is nothing but the material world, then the rational soul is merely the product of atoms and forces. If this is true, then cells retain nothing but the genetic code they had at conception. However, this genetic code could not retain memories essential to making up what we consider “the self.” If there is no memory, then there is no self. If there is no self, there is no intellect to recognize meaning, nor is there any will to attribute meaning.

It sometimes takes years for someone to see the consequences of one’s actions. It sometimes takes decades to see the consequences of one’s ideas. The consequences of the New Atheist movement that exploded in popularity in the early- to mid-2000s have been rearing their ugly head in the soul-crushing nihilism (“black-pilling”) of recent years.

The only way to return oneself to a sense of optimism and hope is to [reestablish transcendence](/2024/05/transcendence-practice-of-music/), or the recognition that something exists beyond what is immediately present to the senses. This inevitably leads to the transcendentals themselves – [truth, goodness, and beauty.](/2024/08/c-s-lewis-true-good-beautiful/) Ultimately, it should lead us to the Source of these transcendentals, what classical philosophy and religion historically recognize as God.
