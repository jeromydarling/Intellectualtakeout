On Nov. 3, what some call a blue tsunami swept Republicans from office at the federal and state levels. Republican politicians and media have unleashed a firestorm of recriminations and blame for their party’s failure at the polls. Many blame Donald Trump, citing the high cost of food and fuel, increases brought on by the Iran war. Some fault Senate Majority Leader John Thune for his failure to pass the SAVE America Act, which many Americans supported. Still others call out the weak candidates fielded in some Republican races. Glaring [discrepancies](https://www.americanthinker.com/blog/2026/11/a-texas-mystery-talarico-got-more-votes-than-hillary-biden-and-kamala/) in voting patterns have also raised eyebrows and accusations.

Yet I’ve read not one article calling out one of the main causes of this political debacle, perhaps from fear of sharing a hard truth, perhaps from simple oversight. These actors in our ongoing political drama don’t sit in Congress, write articles, or blog about politics. No, they are everywhere among us, shopping in grocery stores, talking on their phones, attending universities, and living in retirement communities.

I’m referring, of course, to [American voters](/2026/11/midterms-home-rising-costs-trump/).

More specifically, I’m referring to the historically ignorant, fuzzy-minded, fickle, and foolish American voters. Blend ignorance with brainwashing, ladle on the inability to remember anything beyond yesterday’s headlines, add the spice of arrogance, and you have the recipe for this drubbing.

This description doesn’t apply to all voters. Plenty of citizens enter the voting booth having done their homework. No, what I mean are those uninformed citizens who take their cue at face value from some media commentator or TikTok influencer, the voters who decide communism is cool – “The rich have way too much money” – or who want “free” medical care and housing.

Let’s look at three issues currently in play, which facts and reason easily refute.

### **Communism**

To support communism as a valid form of government in the 21st century is to turn a blind eye to the 20th, when Marxism murdered 100 million people, impoverished countless millions more, and wrecked the dreams of four generations. Yet countless Americans are embracing collectivism.

Arrogance enters when advocates declare that “real communism has never been tried.” Even a cursory knowledge of history demonstrates that it operated around the world with deplorable results. No one has apparently taught them that it wasn’t communism or socialism that lifted so much of the world out of poverty in the last 100 years. It was capitalism.

### **Freebies**

When I see young and old demanding free health care, day care, housing, or anything else, I feel like sitting them down and having them repeat “TANSTAAFL” until it’s burned into their brain. Science fiction writer Robert Heinlein [popularized](https://www.cato.org/blog/who-said-tanstaafl-first) that acronym, which stands for “there ain’t no such thing as a free lunch.” Somebody, usually the taxpayer, is [always footing the bill](/2025/11/self-governance-bedrock-of-liberty/).

Candidates promising to take away the money of billionaires and other Americans call it redistribution, a fancy word for stealing. Voters who support these candidates seem to think that billionaires keep all their money stowed under the bed. Not so. It’s out in the world, employing people and creating opportunity.

### **Schizophrenic Voters**

A Pew Research Poll [shows](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2025/12/04/public-trust-in-government-1958-2025/) American trust in the federal government is at its lowest ebb in almost seven decades, with only 15% of Americans trusting the feds to do the right thing “most of the time.”

Yet the first instinct of many is to turn to the government for economic relief or to settle some cultural quarrel, and they voted to that end in the midterms. They put aside their distrust, voting for candidates promising that more government will make life better, ignoring the ruinous damage inflicted by that same government on health care and education.

And it is that broken government school system which bears the heaviest responsibility for the ignorance found in so many Americans.

In 1983, “[A Nation at Risk: The Imperative for Educational Reform](https://e4e.org/sites/default/files/a_nation_at_risk_1983.pdf)” was released, famously stating: “If an unfriendly foreign power had attempted to impose on America the mediocre educational performance that exists today, we might well have viewed it as an act of war. As it stands, we have allowed this to happen to ourselves.”

The declining test scores of the last few decades and the inability of many students to read or write at grade level reveal that little has changed. That poor education, the neglect of everything from critical thinking to civics, is reflected in our voters, young and old, Republicans and Democrats. We see the anecdotal results as well in those social media clips where some reporter will ask passersby to name the three branches of government, define communism, or identify the First Amendment. All too often, these questions are greeted with baffled looks or wrong answers.

To reverse a trend generations in the making requires the immediate effort of all who believe in liberty and responsibility. Parents, grandparents, and mentors must teach future voters the dangers of a nanny government, truth and reality rather than slogans and ideology, and personal accountability. If possible, parents should also remove their children from government schools and either enroll them in private academies or teach them at home.

We can begin by teaching them TANSTAAFL.
